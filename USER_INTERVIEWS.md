This is one of the strongest signals they read; faking it is an instant reject.**

**3+ direct quotes.**
*Quotes are the load-bearing element of this file. Capture verbatim. They prove the conversation happened and they show what the person actually said versus what you wished they'd said. If you find yourself paraphrasing, the conversation didn't happen.*

> "[Direct quote, with the user's actual phrasing, including any half-finished sentences, hesitations, or domain jargon. Don't sanitize the voice.]"

> "[Second quote — ideally about a specific tool, plan, or dollar number they're tracking.]"

**Most surprising thing they said.**
*The single biggest update to your model of the user. Not "they wanted X feature" — that's a feature request, not a surprise. A surprise is "they don't actually care about [thing you assumed they would care about]" or "they care about [thing you didn't expect to come up]."*

**What it changed about the design.**
*Concrete change: "I removed the X field" / "I added a Y CTA" / "I reframed the headline copy from A to B." Show the cause-and-effect. If nothing changed, that's also worth saying — but most well-conducted interviews change at least one thing.*

## Outreach script you can use

If you need a starting point, here's a script that's worked for me — adapt it to whoever you're DMing:

```
Hi [name] — I'm building a free tool that audits AI tool spend for startups (Cursor, Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT, etc.) and surfaces overspend/cheaper alternatives. It's a 7-day take-home for an internship at Credex. The deliverable is real and live — I'm not pitching you a product.

Would you be up for a 15-minute call this week? I want to understand how you actually decide which AI tools to pay for, what your stack looks like, and what would have to be true for an "audit" to feel useful (vs. another dashboard you ignore). I'll share what I'm building at the end if it's interesting.

No prep on your end. Pick any 15-min slot: [Calendly link or "DM me a couple of times that work"].
```
